## 3. Responsible Disclosure

If you discover vulnerabilities, you are **ethically and legally obligated** to:

### Best Practices
1. **Report privately** to the organization's security team
2. **Don't disclose publicly** until they've had time to fix (typically 90 days)
3. **Document evidence** with proof of vulnerability
4. **Provide remediation guidance** when possible
5. **Follow responsible disclosure timelines** (e.g., CERT/CC guidelines)
6. **Never access more data** than necessary to confirm the vulnerability

### Never
- ❌ Post findings on social media
- ❌ Share with friends/colleagues without permission
- ❌ Demand payment or threaten disclosure
- ❌ Exploit vulnerabilities for personal gain
- ❌ Use for leverage in any transaction
- ❌ Publicly shame organizations before they have time to fix

## 4. Data Protection & Privacy

### Your Obligations
- **Don't exfiltrate data** — Confirm vulnerability without extracting sensitive information
- **Minimize data collection** — Only test what's necessary
- **Protect findings** — Store reports securely with access controls
- **Comply with regulations**:
  - GDPR (EU) — Personal data protection
  - CCPA (California) — Consumer data rights
  - HIPAA (Healthcare) — Protected health information
  - PCI-DSS (Payment) — Credit card data
  - SOX (Finance) — Financial records

### If You Discover Personal Data
- 🔒 Do NOT access, copy, or exfiltrate it
- 🔒 Report the exposure to the organization immediately
- 🔒 Document evidence without storing the data
- 🔒 Consider notifying relevant regulators if required

## 6. Regulatory Compliance

### United States

**Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)**
- Unauthorized access to computer systems is a federal crime
- Penalties: Up to 10 years imprisonment, $250,000+ fines
- Applies to ANY unauthorized testing, even if "no harm" intended

**OWASP/NIST Guidelines**
- Penetration testing requires written authorization
- Must define scope, rules of engagement, and timeline
- Must have incident response procedures in place

### European Union

**GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)**
- Personal data must be protected with appropriate security
- Data breaches must be reported within 72 hours
- Fines up to 4% of global annual revenue or €20 million
- Applies even if testing from outside EU

**Computer Misuse Act (UK)**
- Similar to CFAA in United States
- Unauthorized access is a criminal offense
- Penalties: Up to 10 years imprisonment

### Other Jurisdictions

**Australia** — Computer Crimes Act  
**Canada** — Criminal Code sections 342.1  
**Japan** — Unauthorized Computer Access Law  
**India** — Information Technology Act  
**Singapore** — Computer Misuse Act  
*(Similar laws exist in virtually all countries)*

## 7. Best Practices for Authorized Testing

If you have authorization, follow these practices:

### Pre-Engagement
- ✅ Obtain written authorization (SOW, RFP, etc.)
- ✅ Define scope clearly (URLs, systems, environments)
- ✅ Establish timeline and schedule
- ✅ Identify emergency contacts
- ✅ Agree on incident response procedures
- ✅ Document data handling agreements

### During Engagement
- ✅ Test only authorized systems/URLs
- ✅ Document all activities with timestamps
- ✅ Avoid disrupting services
- ✅ Minimize data exposure
- ✅ Adhere to time windows (don't scan during peak hours)
- ✅ Report critical findings immediately
- ✅ Maintain communication with client

### Post-Engagement
- ✅ Provide comprehensive report with remediation guidance
- ✅ Present findings professionally
- ✅ Offer follow-up testing after fixes
- ✅ Securely destroy findings (per agreement)
- ✅ Maintain confidentiality indefinitely
- ✅ Don't discuss findings without permission

## 8. Ethical Principles

### Core Principles of Ethical Hacking

1. **Permission** — Only test systems you own or have written permission for
2. **Integrity** — Maintain confidentiality and honesty
3. **Responsibility** — Report findings responsibly, minimize damage
4. **Competence** — Use tools properly, understand limitations
5. **Professionalism** — Treat all engagements with professionalism

## 9. Example Authorization Documents

### Simple Email Authorization (Minimum)

```
From: <app-owner@company.com>
To: <security-tester@you.com>
Date: [Date]

Subject: Authorization for Security Testing

I hereby authorize [Your Name/Company] to conduct a security 
assessment of [specific URL/application] from [start date] to [end date].

This authorization covers:
- Active vulnerability scanning
- Payload injection testing  
- Authentication testing
- SSL/TLS analysis

This does NOT cover:
- Denial of service testing
- Data exfiltration
- Social engineering

Scope: [list specific URLs/systems]
Point of Contact: [name, phone, email]
Emergency Contact: [name, phone, email]

Signed: [app-owner name]
Date: [date]
```

### Statement of Work (Recommended for Professional Engagements)

Should include:
- Objectives and scope
- Rules of engagement
- Timeline and availability windows
- Data handling and confidentiality
- Incident response procedures
- Deliverables and reporting
- Fees and payment terms
- Legal jurisdiction and dispute resolution
- Signatures of authorized representatives

## 12. Reporting a Vulnerability You Discover

### The Right Way

```
1. Confirm the vulnerability exists (don't assume)
2. Identify the organization's security contact
   - security.txt (/.well-known/security.txt)
   - security@ email address
   - Privacy policy contact
   - Bug bounty program (if they have one)

3. Send private report including:
   - Description of vulnerability
   - Affected URLs/endpoints
   - Steps to reproduce (without causing damage)
   - Potential impact
   - Suggested remediation
   - Timeline for disclosure (typically 90 days)

4. Do NOT:
   - Post publicly before they fix
   - Sell to third parties
   - Exploit the vulnerability
   - Extract sensitive data
   - Demand payment

5. If no response:
   - Follow responsible disclosure practices
   - Consider notifying CERT/CC or regulators
   - Give reasonable time before public disclosure
```
